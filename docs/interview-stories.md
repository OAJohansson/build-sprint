# Interview stories

A story bank: one real story per product, ready to tell in an interview. Part of the point of 20
products in 20 days is having concrete examples of how I work, not just a portfolio of apps.
Each product's story is its **Interview story** page, written at wrap-up (playbook step 4).

## How to tell one

- **STAR, in about 2 minutes:** Situation (the problem, for whom), Task (what I set out to do and
  how I'd know it worked), Action (the decisions I made and why, including what I assumed and how I
  tested it), Result (what happened, with numbers), then one line on what I learned.
- **Lead with the problem, not the app.** "People Google sunset times, but Google can't tell you
  whether you'll make it" lands better than "I built a sunrise app".
- **Say "I", and be honest about AI.** I made the product calls; Claude helped build and coach.
  Interviewers will ask how you used AI: say what it did well and where you had to correct it.
- **Have the numbers ready:** time to build, test results, what changed after testing.
- **Include a mistake.** The best stories have a wrong assumption or a bug that testing caught,
  and what I changed because of it.
- **Practise the 30-second version.** Most answers start short; the interviewer asks for more.

## Common questions to cover

By day 20, try to have at least one story for each:

| Theme | Typical question |
| --- | --- |
| Discovery | "Tell me about a time you found the real problem behind a request." |
| Prioritisation | "How did you decide what not to build?" |
| Metrics | "How did you know it worked?" |
| Failure | "Tell me about a time you got something wrong." |
| Ambiguity | "Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete information." |
| Trade-offs | "Tell me about a hard trade-off." |
| Craft | "What makes a product great to use?" |
| Technical | "Tell me about a technical problem you solved." |
| Working with AI | "How do you use AI to build products?" |
| Speed | "How do you ship fast without lowering the bar?" |

## The stories

Each story's **Themes** are its main, headline-worthy themes. Smaller examples for every theme are
in the story's *Theme prompts*, so a gap below means "no strong main story yet", not "nothing at all".
