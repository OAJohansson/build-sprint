# User test: CrossFit Log, 2026-10-07

**Persona:** Sam, 32, intermediate CrossFitter (4 yrs, 3–5×/week, kg), high tech comfort, low patience · **Method:** simulated (agent), phone viewport 390×844 · **Build:** commit `bfe20bb`, dev server (in-memory store)

## Verdict
"I'd keep it for the percentages and the voice log. I tap the lift and see 90 kg, or I talk for ten seconds after class and it's logged. But after six weeks it still can't tell me whether I'm getting stronger. Some of the 'PBs' on my board are just my working sets, so I'd stop trusting the board before I'd show it to anyone at the box."

## Value
- **Earns its place:** the percentage grid (weekly, every class; `shots-2026-10-07/percentages-back-squat.png`); voice log with Check & Save (daily); PB celebration (monthly, but it's the emotional hook); calendar dots (a quick weekly look).
- **Doesn't (yet):** the PB board beyond 1RMs (noisy rep maxes, F2); lift history lists (rarely, too much reading); category filter chips (never, the list is short); kg/lb toggle (never, known #8).
- **Missing that would bring them back tomorrow:** a "since you started" line on each lift ("Back squat: 85 → 102.5 kg working weight, +17.5 kg in 7 weeks") and a weekly streak ("3 days this week, 6 of the last 7 weeks").

## Progress
Today Sam can see current bests per lift, a dated list of every time a lift was done, this week's dots and a monthly session count.

Sam can't see whether a lift is trending up or how much it changed over a period. WOD improvements aren't compared: Fran 5:32 → 4:58 are two rows apart on one page. Consistency across weeks isn't visible either: August is two months back, and the missed week of 14–20 Sep only shows as an empty row.

**Most valuable view to add:** a "Progress" section on Home, above the PBs. It answers "Am I getting stronger and showing up?" on one screen:
1. **Consistency strip:** the last 8 weeks as bars of training days ("3, 4, 3, 3, 0, 4, 4, 3"), with "6 of 8 weeks at 3+".
2. **Lifts moving:** for each lift trained 3+ times, a sparkline of the top weight per session and one line: "Back squat +17.5 kg since 18 Aug", "Snatch +10 kg since 20 Aug".
3. **WOD repeats:** benchmarks done twice or more, with the change: "Fran 5:32 → 4:58 (−34 s)".

Tapping a lift opens its page with the same sparkline above the history.

## Top recommendations
1. Add the Progress section on Home (consistency strip, lift changes, benchmark changes). Fixes F6 and partly F5; also answers known #5.
2. Stop turning working sets into rep-max PBs; confirm on review instead. Fixes F2.
3. Derive current bests from all records and keep percentages on the 1RM (or warn loudly). Fixes F3.
4. Make the time field numeric, read "510" as 5:10 and show a preview. Fixes F1.
5. Let a saved session reopen in the Check & Save editor (entries and date), and offer "add to today's class" for a second log on the same day. Fixes F4, F5.

## Method notes
- **Simulated:** the mic (a fake `SpeechRecognition` fed Sam's words) and AI parsing (`/api/parse` intercepted with a 1.5 s delay; in S3 it returned 80 instead of 90). Times are estimates for Sam, not script times.
- **Data:** S1–S4 ran on the empty store. Then 24 sessions were seeded through the API: 17 Aug – 6 Oct, a missed week 14–20 Sep, Fran twice, and one duplicate Fran logged on Sat 3 Oct for S7. Sam's known PBs were entered "today" in S1, so seeded heavy singles (such as the 115 kg squat) weren't counted as PBs. For the same reason, the "3RM 90" from S3 appears in the history list even though a 3RM 100 from 2 Sep is seeded. That ordering is an artefact of the test, but F2 and F3 happen whatever the order.
- **Couldn't judge:** real dictation quality, use in a loud gym, haptics and real device keyboards (F1 is inferred from `inputmode`). The "Sunday" scenarios were run on a Wednesday, so "last Tuesday" = 6 Oct.
- **Suggestion for the testing system:** add a seed file per product (`seed.json` plus a one-line loader) and a "returning user since <date>" option. Seeded history and day-one PBs would then share one timeline, and runs would be comparable.
