# User test: Horizon, 2026-10-07

**Persona:** Noor, 29, remote worker travelling (Bali now, Lisbon next), plans evenings around the sunset · **Method:** simulated (agent), phone viewport 390×844 @2x · **Build:** https://horizon-beta-wine.vercel.app (main @ 2227058)

*Key screenshots, resized, are in `shots-2026-10-07/`. Ran against the live MVP before the
interface-audit fixes, so F1 (contrast) was already fixed when this report came back; see
[Feedback](#02-horizon.feedback).*

## Verdict
"It's lovely. The sky really does look like outside, and '1:30 until sunset' is exactly what I wanted to know, with zero typing. But at 4:45 in the Bali sun I could barely read the white numbers on the pale blue. And the day after I looked up Lisbon, it opened on Lisbon and told me I had 9 hours. I'd keep it for a week, but those two things would push me back to Google."

## Value
- **Earns its place:** The open-and-read countdown, which beats Google for "do I have time?". The live sky is the delight; the sunset strip (s5-sheet-strip.png) is the thing she'd show a friend. Search with local times does job 2 well.
- **Doesn't (yet):** The landmark. It's charming once, but too small to give a sense of place at a glance, and invisible at night. The golden hour row as a bare clock time.
- **Missing that would bring them back tomorrow:** A real sunset moment (F3). "Golden hour in 1 h 01" alongside the sunset countdown. "Will it be a good one?" (cloud, already in the backlog). A reminder ("leave now"), which is out of MVP scope but the obvious next want.

## Progress
Today Noor sees only now: a countdown and today's three times. Nothing shows how sunsets are shifting, what tomorrow looks like, or that she has been catching them. The most valuable addition is a **"This week" strip** below the three rows on the home screen: seven small columns (today plus six days) with the sunset time for the current place and a one-line trend ("Sunset 1 min earlier each day; Lisbon next week: 19:00"). It answers "when should I plan my beach evenings this week?" and makes the app useful when she opens it in the evening on the sofa (moment 2), not only at 4:45.

## Top recommendations
1. Reopen on "my location" when it's allowed; never silently resume a searched city (F2).
2. Make the countdown and label at least 4.5:1 on daytime and golden skies, using a scrim or dark text (F1, F6).
3. Add a "Sunset now / afterglow" state, plus seconds in the final minutes (F3).
4. When the next event is tomorrow, show tomorrow's times and the morning golden hour end; show golden hour as "in 1 h" (F7, F10).
5. Make landmarks bigger, give Tokyo a distinct silhouette, and add a night rim light (F4, F5).

## Method notes
- **Simulated:** geolocation (Seminyak −8.6905, 115.1621, timezone Asia/Makassar) and the browser prompt/deny. Playwright doesn't show the native prompt, so tapping "Use my location" without permission was treated as an immediate deny. City search and reverse geocoding were stubbed with 300 ms delays. "Seminyak" returned nothing only because the stub list lacks it; real Open-Meteo may find it.
- **Clock:** fixed clocks for most screens. R4 was checked with `clock.install` + `runFor` across sunset.
- **Contrast:** measured by sampling screenshot pixels (lightest text pixel against nearby sky), so ±0.2 is approximate.
- **Couldn't judge:** real sunlight glare, animation feel (sun easing, motion while loading), reduce-motion, and real geolocation latency on first open. Times were not checked against timeanddate.com (out of scope), though countdowns were internally consistent.
- **Suggestion for the testing system:** add an automatic contrast check to the harness (sample the countdown bounding box for each sky phase) so the readability guardrail gets a number every run instead of an eyeball judgement.
