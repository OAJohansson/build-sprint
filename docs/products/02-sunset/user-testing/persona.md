# Persona: Noor, a traveller who plans their evenings around the sunset

*Draft by Claude, 7 Oct. Edit to make it yours.*

## Who

- 29, working remotely while travelling for six months; three weeks in Bali now, next stop
  Lisbon. Takes photos on their phone, not a camera.
- Tech comfort high, patience low. Uses Google and the weather app for sunset times today; tried
  a pro photography app once and found it overwhelming.

## Moments of use

1. **Late afternoon, out and about.** Scooter parked, at a café, about 4:45 pm. "Do I have time to
   get to the beach?" A five-second glance, maybe in bright sun.
2. **Evening, planning tomorrow.** On the sofa, relaxed. "When's sunrise for the volcano hike?" Or
   checking a city for next week's trip.
3. **At the beach, waiting.** The sun is low; wants to know how long until it touches the horizon,
   and enjoys the moment.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When it's getting late in the day, I want to know how long until sunset where I am, so I can
   decide whether to go now."
2. "When I'm planning a morning or a trip, I want sunrise and sunset for another place in its
   local time, so I can plan around them."
3. "When I'm about to watch it, I want a calm, beautiful moment, so checking the sunset feels like
   part of the ritual."

## Frustrations

- Typing a search every time for something that should be one glance.
- Doing time-zone maths for another city.
- Dense, ad-heavy pages; pro tools with a hundred settings.
- Apps that ask for location before saying why.

## What "worth it" means

- After a week: opens it instead of Googling; never missed a sunset they meant to catch.
- After a month: it's on the home screen, and they've shown it to a friend at sunset.

## Language they use

Sunset, sunrise, golden hour, "how long have I got?", "what time is it there?". Wouldn't say
civil twilight, azimuth, solar elevation.
