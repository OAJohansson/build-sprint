# Persona: Robin, a would-be writer with ten spare minutes

*Draft by Claude from the owner's own story, 8 Oct. Edit to make it yours.*

## Who

- 32, works in tech. Has always loved storytelling, humour and a well-turned sentence, but long
  believed writing talent is something you're born with. Now treats it as a muscle.
- Journalled for a few months and loved it, until every entry "had to be insightful". Tried
  writing film and book reviews; too big to start, so it stopped.
- Tech comfort high. Writes most on the phone: WhatsApp, Notes. Has tried asking ChatGPT for
  feedback; it rewrote the piece instead.

## Moments of use

1. **On the bus.** Ten minutes, one hand, a bit tired, no idea what to write. Will give up if
   the app asks for decisions first.
2. **In bed.** Relaxed, reflective; the day's small observations are fresh.
3. **Coffee break at work.** Five to ten minutes on the phone or the laptop, between meetings.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I have a spare 10 minutes, I want something small to write about straight away, so I can
   start without deciding what to write or how good it has to be."
2. "When I've written something, I want constructive feedback on my craft, so I can see what
   worked and one thing to try next time."
3. "When I've written for a few weeks, I want to see my output and growth, so I can believe the
   muscle is growing and take on longer pieces."

## Frustrations

- A blank page, or a form to fill in before writing.
- Feeling graded: a score, a list of ten problems, or feedback that rewrites the piece.
- Generic praise ("Great job! Very evocative!"): useless and a bit patronising.
- Losing something they wrote. Any doubt about whether it saved.
- Guilt mechanics: a broken streak, red warnings, "you haven't written in 3 days".

## What "worth it" means

- After a week: wrote four or more times without forcing it; one piece they're quietly proud of.
- After a month: a pile of pieces to scroll back through, and a sense of what they keep getting
  better at. Would tell a friend who "always wanted to write".

## Language they use

Uses: piece, prompt, draft, done, feedback, "what worked", "try next", voice, funny.
Confused or put off by: submission, score, grade, entry, assignment, critique, "AI analysis".
