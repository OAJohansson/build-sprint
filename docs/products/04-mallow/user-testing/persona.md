# Persona: Sam, a long-term pain patient on medical cannabis

Use they/them. Imagined persona (owner, 12 Oct); no real patient tested yet.

## Who

- 44, lives in Leeds, works part-time in admin. Fibromyalgia for six years; tried physio,
  amitriptyline and gabapentin before a private clinic prescribed medical cannabis eight months ago.
- Monthly 15-minute video follow-up with the clinic. Pays for the medicine and the appointments.
- Tech comfort: medium-high. WhatsApp, banking apps, the NHS app. Tried a symptom app once and
  dropped it after a week ("too many questions").
- Patience: low on bad days. Fatigue and brain fog make reading and typing hard.

## Moments of use

1. A quick check-in on the phone, often on the sofa or in bed, sometimes on a bad day with low
   energy and one hand free. No fixed time.
2. The follow-up call: phone in hand or the laptop on the call, a few minutes before or during.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I'm in the follow-up, I want to show what has changed since last time, so the doctor
   adjusts my treatment on what really happened, not on how I feel today."
2. "When I check in, I want to log how today was in a few taps, even on a bad day, so the record
   keeps going without effort."
3. "When I wonder if it's working, I want to see my trend, so I can decide what to ask for."

## Frustrations

- Long questionnaires and forms; anything that feels like homework or a test.
- Typing on a bad day. Small tap targets.
- Apps that guilt-trip: broken streaks, red warnings, "you missed 3 days".
- Feeling disbelieved: pain is invisible and they're tired of justifying it.
- Medical jargon and anything that sounds like an advert for cannabis.

## What "worth it" means

- After a week: checking in feels like nothing, and missed days don't feel like failure.
- After a month: in the follow-up they can say "my sleep is better, pain about the same, dry mouth
  most days" and show it, and the doctor makes a change because of it.
- They'd recommend it if it made them feel taken seriously.

## Language they use

Says: "a bad day", "flare", "sleep was rubbish", "foggy", "the oil", "the flower", "my dose",
"follow-up", "how it's been".
Confused by: "PROMs", "efficacy", "adverse events", "NRS", "baseline", "adherence".
