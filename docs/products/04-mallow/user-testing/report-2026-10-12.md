# User test: Mallow, 2026-10-12

**Persona:** Sam, 44, fibromyalgia, eight months on medical cannabis, monthly 15-minute video follow-up · **Method:** simulated (agent), phone viewport 390×844 · **Build:** branch `04-mallow-test` @ 793557d, local dev server

## Verdict

"Checking in really is nothing, and that summary is the first time I've had something to show the doctor that isn't just me saying 'about the same'. But when my appointment got moved, the summary went blank and the countdown just disappeared. I also can't fix a dose I logged by mistake. That makes me unsure I can trust it on the day that matters." Sam would keep using it for the first month. Whether they still are by the second follow-up depends on F1.

## Value

- **Earns its place:** the check-in (daily), "Took a dose" (daily), the relief question (daily, once F3 is fixed), and the summary (monthly; it's the reason to stay). The countdown, "What you log here is what they'll see", is a real, non-guilting pull.
- **Doesn't (yet):** the calendar is pleasant to look at but answers nothing on its own. The plant is nice but Sam wouldn't notice it growing. Care is used once.
- **Missing that would bring them back tomorrow:** a small payoff after saving, such as "Sleep this week: mostly a bit broken, better than last month". A way to fix yesterday. A gentle optional nudge (out of scope, but without one Sam will forget on bad days).

## Progress

Today Sam can see one month of one symptom as shading, plus a two-period comparison in the summary. Sam can't see the whole eight months, whether last month's dose change did anything, or relief over time.
**Add:** a "Since you started" strip at the top of Journey, under the summary card. One row per tracked symptom, one word per appointment period (Pain: Moderate · Moderate · Mild to moderate), with relief as "quite a bit or more" per period (62% → 78%). Where a note mentions a change ("evening dose an hour earlier"), mark the period.

## Top recommendations

1. Keep the last summary reachable after an appointment passes, and ask about the next date on Today (F1).
2. Fix the dose wording and recheck the relief question on resume (F2, F3).
3. Let Sam edit or delete a dose and its relief, and fill in yesterday (F4).
4. Clinician lines in the summary: medicines and as-needed use, comparison dates, honest tie wording (F5, F6).
5. Accessibility pass: allow partial saves, raise ink-faint and the mild shade to ≥4.5:1 and ≥3:1, pluralise, make the demo band 44 px (F7–F10).

## Method notes

- Simulated: the passage of time. In S1b the dose time was moved back 2 h in `04-mallow:data` and the page reloaded; in a variant Playwright's clock jumped 2 h without a reload. The S4 gap was seeded by writing days into localStorage, and the "appointment passed" variant set `next` to 10 Oct. Date pickers were filled programmatically and counted as about 2 taps. The demo was used for S3. The clinician role was played by the tester.
- Times are estimates for Sam's reading speed, not script times.
- Couldn't judge: real thumb reach, screen readers, whether a real clinician trusts plain-word averages.
- Suggestion for the testing system: add a standard "time passes" step to plans (resume without reload, cross midnight, cross an appointment date). Two of the four majors only show up that way.
