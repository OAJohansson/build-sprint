# Persona: Maya, 19, first-year law student who isn't sure it's hers

Use they/them in reports, even though the demo's Maya is written as "she".

## Who

- 19, first year of a law degree, living in halls. Picked law partly because parents called it
  "the safe choice"; starting to wonder whether it's really hers.
- High tech comfort, low patience. Uses Instagram, TikTok (lots of psychology TikTok), Notes for
  half-started journals, and ChatGPT late at night to talk things through.
- Has never stuck with a journaling app for more than two weeks.

## Moments of use

1. About 10.30 pm, in bed, on her phone, 5–10 minutes. Something that day poked the "am I on the
   right path?" worry. A bit wound up, not distressed. Wants it out of her head before sleep.
2. A random afternoon between lectures, 5 minutes: something's on her mind, no question needed.
3. A Sunday, curious: "what have I actually figured out these last weeks?"

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I'm lying awake looping on whether I'm on the right path, I want to get it out of my
   head and see it more clearly, so I can stop going round in circles."
2. "When I notice something about how I think, I want to know what it is and why people work that
   way, so I feel less weird and more in charge of it."
3. "When I've written a few times, I want to see what I've learned about myself, so it feels like
   it's adding up to something."

## Frustrations

- AI that just agrees with her, or gives a pep talk or a five-step plan.
- Anything that feels like school (lessons, quizzes, scores) or like a therapist's form.
- Blank pages, streak guilt, notifications nagging her.
- Not knowing who reads what she writes.

## What "worth it" means

- After a week: she's left most evenings with one clear sentence about herself and an idea that
  explains it, in plain words, about *her* life.
- After a month: she can see her thinking change, and she'd show a friend the Ideas page.

## Language they use

Uses: "overthinking", "spiralling", "is this normal", "my thing", "vibe", "lowkey".
Confused or put off by: "psychoeducation", "cognitive distortion", "module", "insight bank",
"session", clinical labels about her.

# Persona: Sam, a product hiring manager seeing the demo for the first time

Use they/them.

## Who

- Hiring manager for a product role at a tech company. Has seen many portfolio demos; most are
  pretty screens with no clear reason behind them.
- Opens the demo link on their phone between interviews. Two minutes, maybe three.
- Knows ChatGPT well; doesn't know Inkling or its research.

## Moments of use

1. Just before or after an interview, phone in hand, scanning: "what is this and is it good?"

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I open a candidate's demo, I want to understand what it does and who it's for within a
   minute, so I can judge their product thinking."
2. "When I try it, I want to see a clear journey and some craft, so I can tell it isn't just a
   ChatGPT wrapper."

## Frustrations

- Sign-up walls, broken links, placeholder text, an empty app.
- Not being able to tell what's real and what's mocked.
- Long reading before anything happens.

## What "worth it" means

- They can say in one sentence what makes it different from ChatGPT, and they remember one
  moment from it afterwards.

## Language they use

"value prop", "core loop", "differentiation", "onboarding", "retention", "polish".
