# Persona: the owner, a paper-book reader with a Goodreads past

Use they/them. The owner is the user (owner, 15 Oct): the persona describes them, so the
simulated test checks the owner's own jobs.

## Who

- Thirties, reads regularly (a book every few weeks), paper books, in English as a second
  language.
- Years of history in Goodreads (read and to-read shelves, ratings, some long reviews), but
  avoids opening it.
- High tech comfort, low patience for clunky screens; uses Letterboxd-style apps and likes how
  they look.

## Moments of use

1. Just finished a book, evening, on the sofa or in bed: wants to log it while the feeling is
   fresh, in under a minute.
2. Between books: wants to choose the next one and is open to a suggestion with a reason.
3. Someone mentions a book (a podcast, a friend): wants to save it before forgetting, quickly.
4. First time: wants their Goodreads history in, so the app isn't empty.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I'm between books, I want suggestions that fit my taste, each with a reason, so I pick
   the next one with confidence instead of scrolling."
2. "When I finish a book, I want to rate it and say what I thought in one line, so I remember it
   and my taste record grows without effort."
3. "When I hear about a book I might like, I want to save it in a couple of taps, so it's there
   when I'm choosing."
4. "When I look back, I want to scan my shelf and one-liners at a glance, so I can see my reading
   and remember what each book meant."

## Frustrations

- Dated, cluttered screens (Goodreads).
- Walls of review text you have to read to get the gist.
- Recommendations that are just bestsellers or "more of the same author".
- Starting from zero in a new app.
- Typing a lot on the phone.

## What "worth it" means

After a week: the whole Goodreads history is in, a couple of new books logged with one-liners, and
the shelf is pleasant to scroll. After a month: picked at least one book from the
recommendations and liked it. Would recommend it if it looked as good as Letterboxd and the picks
felt personal.

## Language they use

"Want to read", "read", "stars", "review", "what should I read next", "shelf". Avoid: "items",
"interactions", "candidates", "library sync", "metadata".
