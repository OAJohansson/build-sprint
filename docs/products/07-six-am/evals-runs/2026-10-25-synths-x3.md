## Findings

- **F1 (real, engine): one-tick slip at some tempos.** Identical 909 closed-hat hits, cross-correlated
  against each other, land exactly 60/(BPM × 192) s late on some steps: 90 BPM 3.47 ms (steps 7,
  14, 23, 30 of 32), 126 BPM 2.47 ms, 130 BPM 2.40 ms, 135 BPM 2.31 ms, 174 BPM 1.81 ms; also seen
  on organ/Rhodes and 808 open hat at 90 BPM (raw readings up to 6.2 ms including the detector's
  ~2 ms offset). Clean at 60, 80, 100, 110, 118, 120, 122.4, 124, 128, 140, 150 BPM. Likely cause:
  floating-point tick boundaries in Tone's Transport/Part (PPQ 192) at tempos whose tick isn't
  exact. A slip is always one tick, so it stays under 5 ms above 62.5 BPM; at 60–62 BPM a slip
  would be 4.8–5.2 ms (60 BPM itself tested clean). Worth a fix or a higher PPQ before relying on
  the 60–62 BPM range.
- **F2 (sound design, for the user-tester): some notes aren't re-articulated.** In Reese
  Sixteenths (sub and reese) and Rolling Acid, a quieter note right after a louder one at the same
  pitch has no audible attack (the release tail is louder than the new note's peak), 15–19 per
  render; and 303 slides into the same pitch (Acid Question, by design) make no audible event.
  Pitch and timing aren't wrong; the line just sounds legato there.
- **F3 (headroom):** 808 House Groove peaks at 0.90–0.93 alone; with bass and chords on top the
  Studio mix may clip unless the master has a limiter or the kit gain drops.

## Method

1. Renders: `next dev` + headless Chrome over CDP (no new deps), `window.sixAmSynths` after
   `status === "done"`, WAV pulled in chunks. 32 renders × 5 settings: **A♯ minor 124, F♯ major 122.4,
   C minor 128, E minor 90, D minor 60** (160 renders), plus 909 16th hats alone at 11 more tempos
   for F1. Render time about 10–18 s per setting.
2. Onsets, measured at each intended step from two seeds (grid +8 ms and −8 ms; a reading counts
   only if both agree within 2 ms, so the result doesn't follow the seed), detector per sound:
   - sub: pitch (YIN) + harmonic template fitted after the note, the note's own gain tracked over
     3 ms windows, onset at half its rise; acid/reese: rise of high-frequency energy (2nd
     difference, 1 ms causal), geometric midpoint; organ/Rhodes: same on the full signal; kits: on
     the high-passed signal.
   - **Pad (0.25 s attack): onset = envelope start**, the first sample above 1e-4 (−80 dBFS) after
     ≥ 30 ms of digital silence. Only the first hit of each render qualifies (every later hit swells
     under the previous chord's 1.8 s release), so 15 of 140 pad onsets are measured; the rest share
     the organ/Rhodes code path (`triggerAttackRelease`).
   - Calibrated on synthetic signals with known onsets: sub-style bass ≤ 2.8 ms (+1.5 ms bias from
     the attack), stabs ≤ 0.1 ms, hats ≤ 0.6 ms. Detector offsets are positive (late), so they
     make results look worse, not better.
   - Cross-check: cross-correlation of identical drum hits (sample-exact lags), used to prove F1
     and to clear the 10.3 ms 909 House Groove reading at 128 BPM (open hat at step 34 is 0.00 ms
     from the same sound at steps 2, 18, 50).
3. Pitch: bass YIN median over each note after its attack (after the glide for slides), rounded to
   a semitone, checked against the scale and the intended note. Chords: Hann spectrum of each hit
   (window cut where the hit decays 20 dB, side-lobes of stronger peaks dropped), harmonic sieve
   (a peak within 30 cents of 2–12× a lower fundamental is a harmonic), fundamentals ≥ −20 dB.
   44 "notes" above MIDI 84 in pad renders (≥ 1.1 kHz, quarter-tone off, above the chord voicing)
   are unresolved clusters of the detuned saws' upper partials, not notes; all fundamentals in
   the chord register (< MIDI 80) are in scale, and every intended pad tone is found.
4. Seams: 10 ms energy of pass 1 vs pass 2, and the largest sample jump at the loop boundary vs the
   render's 99.9th percentile. Pass 2's start differs from pass 1's only by the previous pass's
   release tail, which is correct looped behaviour.
