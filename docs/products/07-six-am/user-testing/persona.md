# Persona: the owner, a club-goer turned bedroom producer sorting their SoundCloud

Use they/them. The owner is the user (owner, 18 Oct; again for Ears, 23 Oct): the persona
describes them, so the simulated test checks the owner's own jobs. Updated for Ears v1 on 23 Oct;
the Studio detail from 18 Oct is kept below.

## Who

- Thirties, in the middle of a career pivot. Goes out to house and techno nights and listens a
  lot: the London/Berlin queer underground (Adonis, Gideön, Club Are, Panorama Bar), Chicago and
  NY roots house, emotive vocal house, melodic techno and hard trance.
- Collects on **SoundCloud**: one 56-track dump playlist, *B b baaangers*, being emptied into
  about 15 genre playlists. Most tracks are remixes, edits and bootlegs that can't be downloaded.
- No music training. Knows music by feel, not theory (feels a drop, doesn't know a key or a bar in
  technical terms). Learns by doing and needs concepts in plain words tied to tracks they love.
- Has started producing in the Six AM Studio (since 19 Oct); had closed Ableton, FL Studio and
  GarageBand before that: too much on screen, jargon everywhere.
- High tech comfort (builds web apps with AI coding agents), low patience for cockpits. Likes calm,
  well-designed apps. Values momentum and visible progress over long planning.
- MacBook Pro (Apple silicon), desktop Chrome, headphones.

## Moments of use

1. **Going through the playlist (Ears, main moment).** At the laptop in desktop Chrome, an evening
   or a slow afternoon, SoundCloud playlist open, curious about one track at a time. Headphones
   on, attention on the sound. Has a Claude chat open in another tab. Will grant tab audio once
   and expects it to keep working for the session.
2. **Straight before making something.** A track has just made them want to build one; they want
   the Studio in the same tempo and to start, not to set things up.
3. **Making a track (Studio).** An evening at home, 30–60 minutes free, a track still in their
   head from a night out. Curious and playful, not trying to "learn production".
4. **Coming back.** A few days later, carrying on sorting where they stopped, or playing back last
   time's track.

## Jobs to be done

Ears, ranked:

1. "When I love a SoundCloud track but don't know its genre, I want to play it in Six AM and see
   its top genres, BPM, key and energy, so I can name what I'm hearing."
2. "When I'm sorting my dump playlist, I want the analysis plus a ready-made question for Claude,
   so I can file each track in the right genre playlist."
3. "When a track makes me want to make something, I want to set the Studio to its BPM in one
   click, so I can start in the same tempo."

Studio (from 18 Oct):

4. "When I'm in the mood to make something, I want sounds that already fit together, so I can
   make taste decisions instead of fighting timing and theory."
5. "When I come back, I want to play what I made, so I can hear that I made something real."

## Frustrations

- Uploader tags that lie: "Deep House" on a melodic festival remix; no tags at all on half the
  edits. Hours of artist and label research that still leaves ~10 of 56 tracks uncertain.
- Tools that need a downloaded file, when the music only exists as a stream.
- Being told one genre as fact, when the same song is three genres depending on the remix.
- Permission dialogs they don't understand, or that fail silently (shared, but nothing heard).
- Waiting without knowing for what or how long (a bare spinner during a model download).
- Anything that looks like it's recording or copying the music.
- Screens full of knobs, menus and numbers; jargon (quantise, sidechain, MIDI, LFO, spectral
  centroid).
- Losing work: results gone after a reload.

## What "worth it" means

- After a session: a handful of tracks they can name, each filed in a genre playlist, and one
  thing learned about why (e.g. deep vs progressive: groove vs the slow tease).
- After a week: *B b baaangers* emptied, all 56 tracks placed.
- After a month: they can guess a new track's genre before analysing it and be right more often,
  and they start Studio tracks from tracks they analysed.
- They'd recommend it to a DJ or producer friend if it heard SoundCloud edits nothing else can,
  and was honest when unsure.

## Language they use

- Use: track, edit, remix, bootleg, playlist, genre, vibe, groove, drop, build, bassline, BPM
  (as a number), key (as a label like "F minor", without theory), deep, rolling, hard, warm,
  tag.
- Confusing: spectral centroid, RMS, mel, inference, model, embedding, Discogs style codes
  (`Electronic---Deep House`), "capture" or "record" (sounds like copying), quantise, sidechain,
  MIDI, LFO, DAW. Name concepts the club way: "brightness" (soft/warm vs hard/bright), "energy".
