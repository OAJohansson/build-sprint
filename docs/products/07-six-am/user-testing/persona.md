# Persona: the owner, a house and techno lover who has never produced

Use they/them. The owner is the user (owner, 18 Oct): the persona describes them, so the
simulated test checks the owner's own jobs.

## Who

- Thirties, in the middle of a career pivot. Goes out to house and techno nights and listens a
  lot (Berlin warehouse techno, Chicago house), but has never made a track.
- Had quick looks at Ableton, FL Studio and GarageBand and closed them: too much on screen, jargon
  everywhere, no idea where to start.
- High tech comfort, low patience for cockpits. Likes calm, well-designed apps; knows music by
  feel, not theory (doesn't know what a key or a bar is in technical terms, but feels a drop).

## Moments of use

1. An evening at home on the laptop with headphones, 30–60 minutes free, a track still in their
   head from a night out. Curious and playful, not trying to "learn production".
2. A short 10-minute break: open it, play with a groove, close it.
3. Later the same week: come back to play last time's recording to themselves or a friend.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I'm in the mood to make something, I want sounds that already fit together, so I can
   make taste decisions instead of fighting timing and theory."
2. "When something sounds good, I want to keep it as a track, so the jam isn't lost."
3. "When I come back, I want to play what I made, so I can hear that I made something real."
4. (v2) "When a melody is stuck in my head, I want to hum it in, so the track is really mine."

## Frustrations

- Screens full of knobs, menus and numbers; jargon (quantise, sidechain, MIDI, LFO).
- Anything that sounds wrong or out of time without telling them why.
- Tutorials before they can make a sound.
- Generic loops that sound like a phone game, not like a club.
- Losing work.

## What "worth it" means

- After a week: a couple of recorded jams they'd play back for fun.
- After a month: they feel they *made* these tracks, and want more sounds and the humming.
- They'd recommend it if a friend who "always wanted to make music" could make a groove in five
  minutes and it sounded like house, not a ringtone.

## Language they use

- Use: track, beat, groove, kick, bassline, drop, build, vibe, loop, record, crate.
- Confusing: quantise, BPM (fine as a number, not as a setting to manage), sidechain, MIDI, LFO,
  DAW, bus, gain staging. If a concept is needed, name it the club way ("the pump", "the drop").
