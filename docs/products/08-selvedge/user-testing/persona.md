# Persona: the owner, who dresses well but doesn't speak fashion

Use they/them. The owner is the user (brief): the simulated test checks the owner's own jobs.

## Who

- Thirties, likes dressing well and shopping; knows the brands they already wear, little
  fashion vocabulary beyond that.
- Saves pieces all day on an Android phone (Instagram saves, Pinterest pins, screenshots), so
  their taste is scattered across three apps.
- Sits down with a MacBook to look at things properly. Made a moodboard by hand once (an
  evening in Canva) and won't do it again.
- High tech comfort, a designer's eye: notices clutter, wrong sizes, words that don't fit.

## Moments of use

1. On the phone, mid-scroll, a few seconds, thumb already on Share.
2. On the sofa or at the laptop, 10–20 minutes, curious: "what is my style, actually?"
3. Later in the week: back to the board to tidy it and download it.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I see a piece I like, I want to save it in two taps from wherever I am."
2. "When I sit down, I want all my saves in one place and a board I can make in minutes."
3. "When the board is done, I want to keep it and share it as a picture."

## Frustrations

- Anything that loses a save.
- Clutter: extra buttons, labels that repeat, tape and doodles that pull the eye from the clothes.
- Editors that crop or squash the photos without asking.
- Not knowing whether something is still working (a cut-out, an upload).

## What "worth it" means

- A board in under ten minutes that looks like their own Spring / Summer board, made by hand,
  and that they'd post or set as a wallpaper.
