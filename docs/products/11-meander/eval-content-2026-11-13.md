# Meander content eval: hallucination check (13 Nov 2026)

Grader: `data-evals`. I did not write the content. Graded file: `docs/products/11-meander/content/full.json` (23 schools, 833 gradable items, 2,825 atomic claims). Ground truth: only the saved SEP/IEP corpus (`scratchpad/meander-sources/`, 131 entries). The per-claim log is `eval-content-log.jsonl` in this folder, one line per atomic claim. Each line has the item, the claim, the verdict, the cited sections, the deciding section, the evidence and a suggested fix. Fields `judge_verdict`, `hand_review` and `hand_spot_check` show where I overruled the judge.

## Method
- **Bars 1 and 5, by script.** Every `sources` entry must have a URL in `index.json` and a section heading that exists in that entry with at least 80 characters of text. `"intro"` means the opening text before section 1. The script also checks that `links.json` and the 114 link copies in `full.json` agree, and it greps for `"sample": true`.
- **Bars 2 to 4, by a model judge with a written rubric.** The judge is `claude-opus-5-5`, and the rubric is in `scratchpad/grader/rubric.txt`. There were 168 calls: one per school (description, debatable note, 3 questions, 5 themes, reacted-against), one per thinker (dates, summary, 3 key ideas, study prompt, plus the card check) and one per link. Each item is judged **only against the sections it cites**, with the full section text (and its subsections) pasted in. The judge splits each item into atomic claims and marks each one supported, unsupported or wrong. Being strict about specifics is in the rubric: a dropped hedge or a claim made in an uncited section counts as unsupported.
- **Hand review.** I read all 5 "wrong" verdicts against the source myself and kept 3. I also hand-checked 20 random claims and a further 15 random "unsupported" verdicts to estimate how strict the judge is.

**How strict the judge is.** I hand-checked 15 random "unsupported" verdicts (outside the prompts).
