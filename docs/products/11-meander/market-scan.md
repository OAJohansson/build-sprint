# Market scan

A quick look at what already exists (25 Nov 2026), before deciding whether to grow Meander into something other people use. This is not a full competitor study: one pass of web searches, with each product checked on its own site. Practice: list the **competitive alternatives**, meaning what people would use if Meander didn't exist (April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*). The list includes "do nothing" and "ask a chatbot", not only look-alike apps.

## What Meander is, in one line

A calm map of a whole field. You pick a school and see what it built on, what it pushed against and what grew from it, with a reason and a source for every link. The map remembers where you've been and hands you off to Claude for the deep dive.

## What exists

| Product | What it is | How close | Money |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Map of Philosophy** (map.ebarakos.com) | Free web map of traditions, questions, positions and methods across three connected views, styled as a teal and gold constellation | **Closest in look and scope.** No sign of sources, of tracking where you've been, or of a next step | Free, a hobby project |
| **Kinnu** | Microlearning app with map-based "pathways" across dozens of fields (history, psychology, science…), 5-minute lessons and spaced repetition ("Memory Shield") | **Closest commercial player.** It has a map, many fields and a return habit. The map is a course path, not "how ideas connect" | Free general-knowledge app; 1.2M users, $9M raised; earns from exam prep |
| **Imprint** | Visual, animated lessons drawn from books and courses (philosophy, psychology, economics, history) | Same audience ("I learn visually"), but it teaches linear lessons with no overview of a field | Subscription with in-app purchases; Google Play App of the Year 2023 |
| **Nibble, Chunks, Headway, Blinkist** | Bite-sized lessons or stories across 35+ topics, philosophy and history included | They meet the variety need, with no map | Freemium subscriptions |
| **learn-anything.xyz, BirdsEyes, AI mind-map makers** (Mapify, MyMap and others) | Type any topic and get a generated map | Any topic, instantly, but the map is generated with no check, so you can't trust it | Free to freemium |
| **Chronas and interactive history atlases** | World history on a map and timeline: borders, empires, events | They cover the wider-field idea for history, organised by place and time rather than by ideas | Mostly free |
| **Wine Folly app** (launched Aug 2026), **Sommo**, **ViniGeo**, **The Global Wine Map** | Interactive wine-region maps with grapes, climate and styles. Sommo is a globe that tracks your "wine journey"; ViniGeo links to official sources | **Wine is already crowded**, and its maps are geographic | Wine Folly: free weekly lesson plus paid Plus (32 maps); Sommo is free |
| **Claude or ChatGPT with a Socratic prompt** | What the owner uses today for depth | It does depth well, but gives no overview and no memory of where you've been. It's Meander's partner more than its rival | The user's own subscription |
| **Wikipedia, SEP, IEP, the "History of Philosophy" diagram posters** | Free reference material | Trusted and deep, but in reference order: nothing suggests where to go next | Free |

## What nobody seems to combine

Each of these exists somewhere, but I found no product with all four:

1. **Connections with a reason.** "Built on / pushed against, because…", rather than a tree of categories.
2. **Trust you can check.** Every claim is cited and graded. AI maps don't do this, and of the hobby maps, only ViniGeo links its sources.
3. **A memory of your wandering.** "Your thread" shows where you've been. Sommo and Kinnu track progress, but as a course or a collection.
4. **A hand-off to depth.** Meander gives you a study prompt for your own AI tutor instead of competing with it.

## What this means

- **The idea is not new; the combination is.** A free philosophy map already exists and looks a lot like Clew. If Meander is "a pretty map of philosophy", it has no edge.
- **The edge has to be the hop.** "Shows where to go next, and why, from where you've been" is exactly what's unproven. The day-7 check and the backlog item "next on your thread" test it.
- **Charging for one field is hard.** The close alternatives are free (the hobby map, Kinnu, Wikipedia). People pay for exam prep (Kinnu, WSET wine study), a big library (Imprint) or a habit they can't drop. This gets answered after value is proven, not before.
- **On expansion:**
  - **Wine** is crowded and geographic, so a map of places is already done well.
  - **History** is mostly done by time and place.
  - **Fields of ideas** have a shape that geography can't show: philosophy, art movements, schools of psychology, music genres. That is the shape Meander's globe of connections already draws, so these are the fields where it would be different.
  - **The real cost of a new field is the content pipeline,** not the code: sources, a writer and a grader for each field (about $28 of grading for philosophy).
