# Persona: Sam, a curious self-learner who dives deep and then drops out

Use they/them. Based on the owner (they are the user for this product).

## Who

- Early 30s, in a career change, studying philosophy, psychology and history on their own for fun and meaning. No formal philosophy background; has spent a few weeks on existentialism (Sartre, Nietzsche) through Claude's Socratic modules and quizzes.
- Tech comfort high, patience medium: happy to explore, but leaves anything that feels like homework or a wall of text.
- Remembers what they see far more easily than what they hear or read once.

## Moments of use

1. **Starting a new field:** at a desk, laptop, a free hour, curious and fresh. Wants the shape of the whole thing before reading anything.
2. **Between modules:** after a session on one school, slightly tired of it, wants something connected but different for the next small piece.
3. **Coming back after a few days:** wants to see where they've been and pick up a thread without rereading.

## Jobs to be done

1. "When I get curious about a new field, I want to see its whole shape at a glance, so I know where my interest fits."
2. "When I've spent a while in one area and want variety, I want to see what's connected nearby, so I can switch to something fresh without losing the thread."
3. "When I zoom into an area, I want a short summary of its key questions, themes and thinkers, so I can decide whether to dig in (and then go deeper with Claude)."

## Frustrations

- Long articles that start in the middle of a topic; having to find the next thing to read themselves.
- Linear courses that keep them in one lane until they're bored.
- Jargon without context ("phenomenological reduction") and timelines that are just lists of dates.
- Anything that looks like a school syllabus or a progress scorecard.

## What "worth it" means

- After a week: has touched two or three connected areas instead of one, and can say where existentialism sits in the bigger picture without looking.
- After a month: still studying philosophy, in small varied pieces; the map is the first thing they open before a Claude session.
- Would recommend it if it made a big field feel like a place to wander, not a reading list.

## Language they use

Uses: "big picture", "where does this fit", "what's connected", "go deeper", "jump around", "school", "thinker", "idea".
Confused by: "ontology", "corpus", "node", "graph", "curriculum", "module completion".
